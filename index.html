About the Library

A public library in Rajamahendravaram with a history of more than a century

Sri Gowthami Regional Library began in 1898, when Madhurakavi Nalam Krishna Rao named the library he had founded after Kandukuri Veeresalingam, out of respect for him. It was run for a time at Nalam's Satram, then at the Kaki family's building, and later at the Veeresalingam Town Hall, before prominent figures of that period turned it into a public (Sarvajana) library. By 1900 the library already held about 5,000 books, with 150 readers and 206 members.

In 1919, Vaddadi Subbaraya Kavi's Library, managed by Addanki Satyanarayana Murthy, merged with this public library, and in 1920 the two were registered together under the name Gowthami Library.

In 1979, while Mahidhara Jaganmohana Rao was Secretary, the Government of Andhra Pradesh took over the library. Since then it has been known as “Sri Gowthami Regional Library”. A new building went up in 1987 in place of the old, dilapidated Manduva Logili (traditional courtyard-style) structure; a first floor was added later, and efforts are now underway to add a second floor.

Today the library holds about 1,40,000 books. Around 40,000 of these are very old manuscripts and periodicals, and the library also holds 417 palm-leaf manuscripts.

A building raised over the years

After the Government takeover, a new building went up in 1987 in place of the old Manduva Logili courtyard structure. A first floor was added later, and the library is now working towards a second floor.

Babayamma Memorial Hall rebuilt

When the old hall became unsafe by 2003–04, Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao asked the then city MLA, Dr. Akula Satyanarayana Rao, to rebuild it. ₹75 lakh came via Lok Sabha member Sri Maganti Murali Mohan and ₹2 crore from Chief Minister Sri Nara Chandrababu Naidu's special funds. The new building cost ₹2.75 crore in all, and the Rajahmundry Municipal Corporation furnished it with ₹30 lakh worth of furniture.

Ancient books and periodicals digitised

Digitisation began in 2017 with Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao's support. From 2019 he has funded the salaries of three staff members digitising books and periodicals from before 1960, while local organisations and donors have contributed the equipment needed.

Babayamma Memorial Hall

Built in 1927 in memory of Babayamma

Babayamma was the daughter of Kanchumarti Sitarama Chandra Rao. A child widow, she passed away at about 25 or 26. She had studied up to matriculation, and while serving as a municipal councillor she was also selected as a magistrate. In 1927, her father, zamindar Sitarama Chandra Rao, gave ₹6,000 to Sri Gowthami Library's governing body to build a hall in her memory.

The Babayamma Memorial Hall became a hub for literary, cultural and women's programmes. The ceremony honouring Sri Sripada Krishnamurthy Sastry with the “Suvarna Gandapenderam” — a prestigious golden-anklet honour — was held in this hall.

The hall had fallen into disrepair by 2003–04. Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao, in his personal capacity, asked the then city MLA, Dr. Akula Satyanarayana Rao, to build a new one in its place. The MLA secured ₹75 lakh from the then Lok Sabha member, Sri Maganti Murali Mohan, and Chief Minister Sri Nara Chandrababu Naidu later sanctioned ₹2 crore from his special funds. The new building was built at a cost of ₹2.75 crore, and the Rajahmundry Municipal Corporation provided furniture worth ₹30 lakh.

History

From a small library in 1898 to the Sri Gowthami Regional Library

1898

The library is founded and named

Madhurakavi Nalam Krishna Rao named the library he had founded after Kandukuri Veeresalingam, out of respect for him.

It was run for a time at Nalam's Satram, then at the Kaki family's building, and later at the Veeresalingam Town Hall. Prominent figures of that period turned it into a public (Sarvajana) library.

1900

An early record of readership

Records show the library already held about 5,000 books, with 150 readers and 206 members.

1919–20

Merger and registration as Gowthami Library

Vaddadi Subbaraya Kavi's Library, managed by Addanki Satyanarayana Murthy, merged with the public library in 1919. In 1920 the two were registered together as Gowthami Library.

1922–23

A site of its own

In 1922 the library moved to a site belonging to Barrister Garimella Bapanna Pantulu. In 1923, a plot of 3,100 square yards was purchased and registered for ₹13,500.

Nyapathi Subbarao Pantulu and Kanchumarti Sitarama Chandra Rao helped in buying the site.

1927

Babayamma Memorial Hall

Zamindar Kanchumarti Sitarama Chandra Rao gave ₹6,000 to build a hall for the library in memory of his daughter Babayamma, who died at a very young age.

1979

The Government of Andhra Pradesh takes over

Many eminent people formed the governing body and developed the library; committee members such as Tenneti Ramashankaram and Kocharlakota Venkateswarlu rendered notable service. While Mahidhara Jaganmohana Rao was Secretary, the Government of Andhra Pradesh took over Sri Gowthami Library in 1979. Since then it has been known as “Sri Gowthami Regional Library”.

Freedom fighters Vavilala Gopalakrishnayya and Krovvidi Lingaraju, former MLA Pothula Veerabhadra Rao, Varada Brahmanandam, and Y. S. Narasimha Rao of the Andhra Kesari Yuvajana Samithi, among others, played a key role in the Government's takeover of the library.

1987

A new building replaces the old courtyard structure

Following the takeover, a new building was constructed in 1987 in place of the earlier, dilapidated Manduva Logili (traditional courtyard-style) structure. A first floor was added later, and in recent times efforts have been made to add a second floor.

2003–04

Babayamma Memorial Hall becomes unsafe

By 2003–2004 the Babayamma Memorial Hall had fallen into disrepair. Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao, in his personal capacity, asked the then city MLA, Dr. Akula Satyanarayana Rao, to build a new one in its place.

Rebuilt hall

Babayamma Hall rebuilt for ₹2.75 crore

Dr. Akula Satyanarayana Rao responded promptly and secured ₹75 lakh from the then Lok Sabha member, Sri Maganti Murali Mohan. Chief Minister Sri Nara Chandrababu Naidu then sanctioned ₹2 crore from his special funds.

The new building was constructed at a total cost of ₹2.75 crore, and the Rajahmundry Municipal Corporation furnished it with ₹30 lakh worth of furniture.

2017

Digitisation begins

Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao approached Dr. Akula Satyanarayana Rao with the idea of digitising ancient texts and housing them in the new building for future generations. Digitisation began with Dr. Aripirala's financial support.

2019 onwards

Books and periodicals before 1960 are digitised

Since 2019, Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao has funded the salaries of three staff members digitising ancient books and periodicals from before 1960.

At his appeal, numerous local organisations and philanthropists contributed the equipment needed for the project, and generous individuals from the town continue to offer voluntary financial support.

2024

A grant from RUDA

The Rajahmundry Urban Development Authority (RUDA) sanctioned a grant of ₹33 lakh in 2024 for the development of the library.

Recent

Continuing support

Rajahmundry Member of Parliament Smt. Daggubati Purandeswari has been facilitating further grants and assisting the library's ongoing development.
