About the Library

A public library in Rajahmundry with a history of more than a century

Sri Gautami Regional Library began in 1898, when Madhurakavi Nalam Krishna Rao named the library he had established after Kandukuri Veeresalingam, out of respect for him. The elders of that time made it a public library, and in 1920 it was registered as Gautami Library.

In 1979 the Government of Andhra Pradesh took over the library. Since then it has been known as “Sri Gautami Regional Library”.

Today the library has about 1,40,000 books. About 40,000 of them are very ancient books, handwritten copies and periodicals, and the library also has 417 palm-leaf manuscripts. Ancient books and periodicals from before 1960 have been digitised so that they remain useful for future generations.

History

From a small library in 1898 to the Sri Gautami Regional Library

1898

The library is founded and named

Madhurakavi Nalam Krishna Rao named the library he had established after Kandukuri Veeresalingam, out of respect for him.

For some time it was run at Nalam’s satram, then at Kakivari Bhavanam, and later at Veeresalingam Puramandiram. The elders of that time converted the Veeresalingam Library into the Sarvajana Library.

1920

Registered as Gautami Library

Addanki Satyanarayana Murthy had established a library in the name of Vaddadi Subbarayakavi (the Vasuraya Library). The Sarvajana Library and the Vasuraya Library were joined and registered as Gautami Library in 1920.

1922–23

A site of its own

In 1922 the library moved to the site of Garimella Bapanna Pantulu. In 1923, ₹13,500 was paid and 3,100 square yards were registered.

Nyapati Subbarao Pantulu and Kanchumarti Sitarama Chandra Rao helped in buying the site.

1927

Babaiyamma Memorial Hall

Kanchumarti Sitarama Chandra Rao built a building for the library with ₹6,000, in the name of his daughter Babaiyamma, who died at a very young age.

1979

The Government of Andhra Pradesh takes over

Many distinguished people formed the governing body and developed the library. When Mahidhara Jaganmohana Rao was Secretary, the Government of Andhra Pradesh took over Sri Gautami Library in 1979. Since then it has been known as “Sri Gautami Regional Library”.

Freedom fighters Vavilala Gopalakrishnayya and Krovvidi Lingaraju, former legislators Sri Potula Veerabhadra Rao and Sri Varada Brahmananda, and Sri Y. S. Narasimha Rao of the Andhra Kesari Yuvajana Samiti, among others, worked to have the library taken over by the government.

2003–04

The old hall becomes unsafe

By 2003–2004 the Babaiyamma Memorial Hall had become dilapidated. Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao asked the then city legislator Dr. Akula Satyanarayana to have a new building built in its place.

New building

A two-storey building for ₹2 crore 75 lakh

Dr. Akula Satyanarayana responded at once and got ₹75 lakh sanctioned through the then Lok Sabha member Sri Maganti Murali Mohan. The then Chief Minister Sri Nara Chandrababu Naidu later sanctioned ₹2 crore from the Chief Minister’s special funds.

The two-storey building, each floor about 5,600 sq ft, was built with the total of ₹2 crore 75 lakh. Dr. Akula Satyanarayana handed over the responsibility of completing the construction to Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao.

2017

Digitisation begins

Along with the construction of the modern building, Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao felt that ancient books should be digitised and kept in the new building for future generations. At his request, digitisation began first with the financial help of Dr. Akula Satyanarayana.

2019 onwards

Books and periodicals before 1960 are digitised

From 2019, Dr. Aripirala Narayana Rao gave ₹20,000 every month from his pension as wages. With the help of three young people, ancient books and periodicals from before 1960 were digitised.

At his call, many institutions and donors in the city arranged the equipment needed for digitisation. He is working to keep the digitised books on the internet.

Babaiyamma Memorial Hall

Built in 1927 in memory of Babaiyamma

Babaiyamma was the daughter of Kanchumarti Sitarama Chandra Rao. She was widowed in childhood and died at the age of about 25 or 26. In her name, Sitarama Chandra Rao, a zamindar, gave ₹6,000 in 1927 to the governing body of Sri Gautami Library for the construction of a building.

Babaiyamma had studied up to matriculation. While she was a municipal councillor, she was also selected as a magistrate.

The Babaiyamma Memorial Hall became a centre for literary, cultural and women’s programmes. The honour of the Suvarna Gandapendera for Sri Sripada Krishnamurthy Sastry was held in this hall. By 2003–04 the hall had become dilapidated, and a new library building was built in its place.
